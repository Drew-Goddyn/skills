# Review and handoffs

Read before the first consequential candidate review. Use one capable reviewer by default, not a panel. Keep the core loop and its decision meanings in SKILL.md authoritative.

## Arrange real independence

Give a fresh-context reviewer the chosen brief, target, protected baseline, best checkpoint, candidate identity, launch instructions, and required observation. Start with artifacts and user-reported symptoms rather than the builder's success narrative. Let the reviewer form an initial judgment before consulting previous findings for continuity.

A separate pass in the builder's existing conversation is self-review. Fresh review is required for realization by default. When it is unavailable, label that limit and do the available self-checks; only the human can waive the requirement. Missing required independence or observation blocks the corresponding readiness claim; it does not prohibit unrelated bounded investigation.

A reviewer reports what it actually inspected. Repository access is not runtime access, saved images are not viewed images, sampled frames are not continuous playback, and a recording cannot establish hands-on enjoyment.

## Inspect the complete small experience

Compare with both the target and the best checkpoint. Ask:

- Does the intended experience actually happen, at the intended visual and behavioral quality?
- What defining gaps remain? Identify visible or audible symptoms before suggesting causes.
- Did the change preserve the agreed baseline, including ordinary use after the new interaction?

For appearance, inspect framing, composition, scale, lighting, materials, and decisive details at intended viewing size. For interaction or motion, observe the primary action through its aftermath and an important variation, interruption, or repeat when relevant. For a static deliverable, do not invent interaction requirements.

Hold nonexperimental view, state, scale, and capture settings steady. Identify intentional changes. Verify the intended candidate and assets loaded, including uncommitted changes when a commit alone is insufficient.

Use actual audio for listening claims and matched measurements for performance claims. Passing tests cannot substitute for seeing motion. A passing brief outing and a failing stress case can both be true. A later pass does not erase an earlier failure or prove its cause.

## Return a decision that drives work

Return a compact report:

1. Candidate and evidence actually inspected, plus unobserved requirements.
2. Defining criteria met or unmet, with concrete observations or timestamps.
3. Important gains and regressions against the target and checkpoint.
4. A recommendation using CONTINUE, READY, REJECT, or PAUSE as defined in SKILL.md, with the most important remaining gaps.

Recommend CONTINUE when a promising improvement is still unfinished and useful work fits the budget. Readiness needs the scoped experience, not merely proof of the idea. Scores may support comparison within a review, but never replace criteria or become a calibrated progress series across different reviewers. No average cancels a failed core requirement.

A failed first implementation may need better realization rather than rejection of the idea. Repeated symptoms may require a diagnostic or a new approach rather than another small adjustment. Keep causes tentative until the available evidence distinguishes them.

Resolve material reviewer disagreements with a focused check, not repeated reviews until someone approves. The human retains taste and acceptance authority unless delegated; ask for play when their experience is genuinely needed, not for routine technical diagnosis.

## Hand over only what helps the decision

For a build milestone, provide the runnable location or exact revision, target, one useful comparison or short uncut recording, review findings, and limits. Link existing recoverable artifacts instead of packaging the whole history every round.

For a next creative pass, provide the accepted baseline, its new capabilities and limitations, representative captures, and the few graph links that explain useful possibilities. Keep rejected work as evidence, not an assignment list.
