# A Small Visitor: source-grounded example

This illustrates the process using published Dreams records. It is not a run of the rebuilt skill, a complete reconstruction of the original session, or a claim that every part of the encounter passed.

## From existing experience to chosen direction

Kip already moved, recovered from falls, carried a plant, and disturbed leaves. The selected addition was a small bird that could approach and rest on his hat. Familiar movement could become something to attempt gently because another creature was nearby.

The saved builder brief explicitly closed concept selection. It supplied a generated target, preserved Kip and the garden, required the established asset-authoring process, and asked a fresh critic to inspect approach, response, perching, interruption, and re-approach. A cute standing-perch frame was insufficient.

## Why the first apparent success was not the finish

The chapter records an encounter whose returned bird could disappear among leaves or behind Kip. The bird's return existed, but the experience was hard to read. A focused follow-up gave it a visible place to land without redesigning the garden or enlarging the bird. The recorded follow-up also included a careful step and turn while perched and another approach without reset.

Under this skill's decision language, a promising implementation with an unreadable return calls for CONTINUE, not READY. The useful checkpoint is saved while the defining presentation gap is repaired. This is an interpretation of the records, not a historical verdict quoted from the original agents.

## What can be inherited honestly

The published account describes an accepted staging follow-up but retains limits: sampled-frame review, silent evidence, an earlier unremeasured stall, and known issues involving the pot, ledge recovery, and leaves. The records do not independently establish hands-on enjoyment or audio quality.

A compact graph could connect the existing movement, carrying, and leaves to the selected visitor, then the follow-up's readable return. A later idea may build on that demonstrated encounter while carrying the known limits forward. Any proposed next interaction is a new possibility, not an already validated capability.

The same distinction appeared in the first Kip build: visual reviews reached their target while movement reviews still rejected the result. That supports separate appearance and behavior checks; it does not establish that this rewritten skill reliably produces better work.
