# Visual convergence — draft

A decision procedure from a mixed-outcome project, not a proven photorealism recipe. Use the user's current scope, acceptance authority and budget. The procedure does not authorize purchases, replacement targets, new frameworks or publication. Read the [case study](references/session-lessons.md) when auditing a rule's support or adapting the process after a stall.

## 1. Define what must improve and what must survive

Identify the actual retained artifact, including uncommitted changes, and preserve a recoverable, inspectable artifact. Express accepted behavior as visible outcomes and meaningful input sequences; implementation files are evidence, not the entire contract. Record the quality sought, protected behavior, allowed changes, available tools, and an explicit iteration budget. If the user supplied no budget, state a bounded initial attempt within the authorized scope.

Choose reference states and stressful combinations across the relevant range: view, lighting, material parameters, motion and input history can all matter. Select combinations by changed properties and likely failures. Separate observations required for the next retention decision from those needed for final acceptance. Keep approved expectations stable. Mark provisional references, approximate registration and contradictions; seek a consequential decision when the target cannot express the requested behavior rather than silently changing it.

**Done when:** the baseline is recoverable and the comparison states, expected gain, protected outcomes and budget are recorded. [E2](references/session-lessons.md#e2), [E4](references/session-lessons.md#e4), [E7](references/session-lessons.md#e7).

## 2. Qualify the representation only where doubt remains

Before investing heavily in an uncertain representation, test its hardest required state in the actual deliverable. When interaction is required, exercise interruption and identify which systems must vary independently or share continuous state. A promising image is insufficient if its representation cannot support the required experience. Preserve an already qualified base while testing alternatives.

For imported or converted assets, compare a reproducible source appearance, unchanged runtime state and target before combining conversion repair with deformation changes. Distinguish poor source quality from lost shading, detail or motion during conversion. Choose tools and assets within existing permissions, based on this evidence.

**Done when:** the uncertain capability has a demonstrated result or a named limitation; the alternative has not silently displaced the retained base. [E1](references/session-lessons.md#e1), [E3](references/session-lessons.md#e3), [E4](references/session-lessons.md#e4).

## 3. Design a test that can change the next decision

Choose the purpose. For a **diagnostic**, name the visible defect, competing causes, discriminating result and held conditions; use a controlled test and verify the mechanism works before judging its image. For a **candidate**, name the intended whole-result gain and the regression that would disqualify it. Coordinated changes can be appropriate. Allow incomplete intermediate states without promoting them or demanding that every edit beat the baseline. Judge the complete result at intended viewing size; a clearer feature can make the whole image worse.

**Done when:** the diagnostic can distinguish causes, or the candidate has a checkable gain/regression criterion, within the remaining budget. [E5](references/session-lessons.md#e5), [E6](references/session-lessons.md#e6).

## 4. Capture evidence for the claim being made

Use the actual deliverable. Hold nonexperimental framing, state, scale and output settings fixed; declare intentional changes. Verify that the correct asset/version loaded and that comparisons share the intended state. Identify evidence by source and asset hashes when a commit alone does not describe the captured working tree.

Match evidence to the claim: stills for appearance at sampled states; original timed playback and interruption windows for visible motion; inputs and traces for control/recovery; source-to-runtime comparisons for conversion. Run affected existing checks. If playback or device access is unavailable, keep that claim unobserved and provide the strongest available evidence with its limits. An unobserved requirement blocks a decision that depends on it; other authorized work can continue within budget. Protected outcomes are not waived. Regular callbacks and passing tests cannot substitute for seeing the motion.

**Done when:** comparisons are valid and each material claim is supported, contradicted or explicitly unobserved. [E2](references/session-lessons.md#e2), [E8](references/session-lessons.md#e8), [E10](references/session-lessons.md#e10).

## 5. Review the artifact before the explanation

For consequential retention, use an independent reviewer when available. Provide expectations, retained best, candidate, required states and evidence limits before the builder's explanation. A blind review requires a clean context without inherited success commentary; otherwise describe it accurately as informed independent review or self-review.

Request per-state gains and regressions, cross-state problems, whole-result direction, and inspected versus unobserved evidence. Ask for visible symptoms first; proposed causes remain hypotheses. Compare with both the expectation and retained best, not only the preceding experiment. Keep incremental retention, functional correctness and final human acceptance separate. Scores from different reviewers are not a calibrated progress series.

**Done when:** the retention decision is traceable to observable differences and protected outcomes, with disagreement exposed. [E4](references/session-lessons.md#e4), [E5](references/session-lessons.md#e5), [E9](references/session-lessons.md#e9).

## 6. Retain, revert, continue or pause

- **Retain** a completed candidate when it meets the agreed visible-gain criterion and preserves required outcomes. Record remaining defects and observation limits; retention is not final acceptance.
- **Revert** a completed regression or no-gain candidate to the retained best. Preserve useful negative evidence and its tested conditions.
- **Continue within budget** when a useful diagnostic or justified coherent candidate can add information or pursue the agreed gain. Repeated symptoms demand a new question or approach, not another unexplained micro-adjustment. Apply the user's actual stop rule; this draft supplies no universal round count.
- **Pause for review** when the authorized budget ends, the next useful action depends on unavailable evidence or new authority, or the result presents a consequential tradeoff against protected behavior. Show the limitation and the decision needed.

Finish when the requested acceptance criterion and applicable checks are satisfied under the designated acceptance authority. Lead the handoff with one useful comparison and what changed. Distinguish visible progress, diagnostic learning and reproducibility work. Keep source and decisive evidence recoverable; avoid duplicating a bulky history into every handoff.

**Done when:** the selected artifact, reason, recovery point and next boundary are clear. [E6](references/session-lessons.md#e6), [E7](references/session-lessons.md#e7), [E10](references/session-lessons.md#e10).

The loop structure draws on Dream Loop Pro; the multi-state contract, acceptance separation and local stopping decisions are adaptations. [Attribution and limits](references/session-lessons.md#attribution).
